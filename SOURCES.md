# Sources and confidence

The animal names (words) are ordinary vocabulary. The **sounds** are the risky part:
every language writes animal noises differently, and no single authoritative list
covers all 12 languages and 20 animals. This is where each sound came from.

## Checked against a published table

[Cross-linguistic onomatopoeias, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-linguistic_onomatopoeias)
has per-language tables (domestic birds, wild birds, cats and dogs, equine, cloven-hoofed,
other mammals, other animals). Sounds were compared against it for:

- **English, Polish, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Japanese, Mandarin, Arabic**:
  corrected wherever the page disagreed (for example Spanish duck *cua cua*, Spanish
  horse *ji ji*, French frog *croa croa*, Italian pig *oink oink*, Polish turkey *gul gul*).
- **Welsh**: only cat, cow, sheep and pig appear there, plus an informal
  [Say Something in Welsh forum thread](https://en.forum.saysomethingin.com/t/welsh-onomatopoeia/3224)
  (duck *cwac*, frog *crawc*; it also gave dog *wff wff*, which has since been removed as doubtful). The forum author says his Welsh is rusty
  and that not all of it is accurate.

That Wikipedia article carries its own "needs more citations" banner, so a match is
*supporting evidence*, not proof.

## English notes

- Cat: Wikipedia gives *miaow* (UK) and *meow* (US). The site uses *meow*.
- Frog: Wikipedia gives *ribbit* for the US and *croak* for Britain. The site uses *ribbit ribbit*, the form children expect.

## Sounds left blank on purpose

Sounds I could not back with a source were **removed**, not guessed. Those animals show the
word only, ready for a native speaker to add (the "+ Add" buttons on the site, or send a list).

- **Urdu and Punjabi**: only dog, cat and mouse have a sound. Wikipedia has almost nothing, and
  the Ling App pages I found ([Urdu](https://ling-app.com/blog/urdu-animal-sounds/),
  [Punjabi](https://ling-app.com/blog/punjabi-onomatopoeia/)) are clearly machine-translated
  and unreliable (for example, plural nouns given as the sound).
- **Welsh**: kept cat, cow, sheep, duck, pig, frog, rooster (Wikipedia, the forum thread
  above, Omniglot), and lion and elephant *rhuo*
  ([We Learn Welsh](https://welearnwelsh.com/words/welsh-word-rhuo-to-roar/)). The other ten
  are blank.
- **Arabic** (8 blank), **Chinese** (4), **French**, **German** (3 each), **Italian**, **Spanish**
  (2 each) and **Japanese** (1): removed where neither Wikipedia's tables nor another source
  confirmed the sound.
- **Mouse**: the sources found were blog lists that disagree, so treat it as unverified.

A few older entries flagged `c:true` in `index.html` (from the original site) still show a
"Community" tag. Please have a native speaker confirm those.

## Pronunciation hints

The small text under each animal name (for example *hooi-AH-den*) is a plain-English
respelling that I wrote, using Wiktionary's IPA for the word as a check where it had one
(about 190 of the 220 words). It is a rough guide for English speakers, not an
authoritative transcription, and has **not** been checked by native speakers. Urdu and
Punjabi are the least certain (several words had no IPA, and Punjabi tone changes the
sound of some letters), and Mandarin tones are not shown. English words and the animal
*sounds* have no hints. Respellings sit in the `r:` field of each word in `index.html`.
